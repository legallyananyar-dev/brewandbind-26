CADD has two branches: structure-based (docking, MD) and ligand-based (QSAR, pharmacophore, similarity).

Docking predicts how a ligand fits into a protein’s binding site and how strongly.

1. Search
Samples orientations, positions and conformations (poses) in the site.
2. Scoring
Estimates binding energy (Vina: kcal/mol, more negative = better) to rank poses.
Scores are estimates for ranking, not measured affinities. Always inspect poses and validate.

They complement each other: docking pre-filters a huge library so a small, enriched set goes to the lab.

Library (ZINC, ChEMBL, PubChem)→Property filters (Lipinski, PAINS)→Fast screening→Top hits→Careful docking of each→Rescoring, MD, visual check→Wet lab

mass-screen cheaply, keep the top fraction, then dock each into the target with higher exhaustiveness. Each stage shrinks the set so expensive steps run on fewer molecules.

  1. Define the target: pick, clean, add hydrogens, save as PDBQT.
  2. Define the ligand(s): 3D coordinates, pH 7.4, charges, PDBQT.
  3. Define the box around the binding site.
  4. Run docking (exhaustiveness, number of poses).
  5. Analyse: rank, check H-bonds and hydrophobic contacts.
  6. Validate: redock the native ligand; RMSD under about 2 Å.

Vina only searches inside the box. A box on the real site is faster, more accurate and more reproducible than searching the whole protein.

With a co-crystal ligand (easy): center = geometric center of the bound ligand’s atoms. Size = ligand extent + about 8–10 Å padding (often 20–25 Å per side).

Co-crystallized: the PDB file contains a HETATM ligand in the pocket; its centroid gives the box center.

Try it: box size

Apo: no HETATM to copy coordinates from, so you must predict the site. Pockets can also differ in shape without a ligand. Schematic, not real coordinates.

fpocket is a geometry-based pocket detector. It finds cavities on the surface (Voronoi tessellation and alpha spheres), ranks them by druggability-related descriptors, and outputs pocket atoms and centers. Use the top pocket’s center and extent as your box. It tells you where to dock, not how strongly.

Other options: P2Rank (machine learning), CASTp, literature or UniProt residues, a homolog with a ligand, or blind docking with a whole-protein box and higher exhaustiveness, then redock into the best site.

DiffDock uses diffusion models trained on protein-ligand complexes to propose poses; GNINA adds CNN scoring on top of classical sampling.

Uses
Blind docking without a box, fast pose proposals, better rescoring of Vina poses, some receptor flexibility.
Caveats
May generalise poorly to novel pockets, give strained poses, and confidence is not affinity. Refine and rescore with physics-based methods.
